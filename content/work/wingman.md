## The problem

Wingman was a dating app built on peer perspective — your friends vouching for you rather than you selling yourself. It worked, and it had users. But it had been built end to end by an offshore development agency, and that agency still held the app, the backend, the website and the source.

That is a worse position than it sounds. Every change went through someone else's backlog at someone else's price. Nobody inside the company could answer a technical question without asking permission first. And the cost of leaving grew every month the arrangement continued.

## What I did

I came in as CTO and product lead, and onshored the whole thing.

- **Extracted the full stack from the vendor** — app, backend, website and source — without interrupting the live product or its users.
- **Migrated everything onto infrastructure the company owned and controlled**, so deployment, data and access were ours.
- **Rebuilt the release and deployment process in-house**, which turned shipping from a negotiation into a decision.
- **Hired and led the engineering team** that took over day-to-day development.
- **Scaled the product internationally**, with reliability as the explicit priority — a dating product that is down at the wrong moment does not get a second chance.

## The outcome

Wingman owned its own technology, could ship independently, and grew into new markets on that footing. It ran until the company's funding ran out in September 2025.

The company not surviving is a separate story from the engineering one. If you are currently dependent on an agency that holds your source, this is the specific problem I have solved before, end to end.
