## The problem

Wingman was a social app built on peer perspective — your friends vouching for you. It worked, and it had users. But it had been built end to end by an offshore development agency, and that agency still held the app, the backend, the website and the source.

That is a worse position than it sounds. Every change went through someone else's backlog at someone else's price. Nobody inside the company could answer a technical question without asking permission first. And the option of simply leaving got more expensive every month the arrangement continued.

## What I did

I came in as CTO and got the product back.

- **Extracted the full stack from the vendor** — app, backend, website and source — without interrupting the live product or its users.
- **Migrated everything onto infrastructure the company owned and controlled**, so deployment, data and access were ours.
- **Rebuilt the release and deployment process in-house**, which turned shipping from a negotiation into a decision.
- **Hired and led the engineering team** that took over day-to-day development.
- **Took the product forward** on that footing, improving it from a position where we could actually make changes.

## The outcome

Wingman owned its own technology and could ship independently, and ran on that basis until the company's funding ran out in September 2025.

The company not surviving is a separate story from the engineering one. If you are currently reliant on an agency that holds your source, this is the specific problem I have solved before, end to end.
