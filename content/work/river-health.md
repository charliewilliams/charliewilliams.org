## The problem

River Health is a telehealth platform. Two things are true of almost every product in that category: the security requirements are non-negotiable, and the people using it are often unwell, in a hurry, or unfamiliar with the app.

Those two pressures pull against each other. Security work tends to add steps; patient usability demands fewer. Getting both right at once is the actual job.

## What I did

I was the sole iOS developer on the product for three years, which meant owning the app end to end — architecture, delivery, releases and the App Store relationship.

- **Rebuilt the app** to raise its security posture, in a domain where handling patient data correctly is a requirement rather than a preference.
- **Reworked the user experience** so the product was navigable by patients under real conditions, not only by clinicians who use it every day.
- Maintained continuity across three years, which in a regulated product is worth as much as any individual feature.

## The outcome

A telehealth app that met a materially higher security bar and was easier for patients to actually use, maintained by one person across three years of platform changes.

Regulated products are a good fit for how I work: they reward people who have shipped through App Store review repeatedly and who treat privacy and data handling as design constraints from the start rather than as a compliance pass at the end.
