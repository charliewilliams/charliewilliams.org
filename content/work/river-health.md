## The problem

River Health is a telehealth platform connecting clinicians and patients. Two things are true of almost everything in that category: the privacy requirements are non-negotiable, and the people using it are often unwell, in a hurry, or unfamiliar with the app.

Those pressures pull against each other. Security work tends to add steps; patient usability demands fewer. Getting both right at once is the actual job.

There is a third constraint that is easy to underrate: uptime. If a clinician cannot reach a patient at the appointed time, the product has failed regardless of how well anything else works.

## What I did

I was the sole iOS developer on the product, which meant owning the app end to end: architecture, delivery, releases and the App Store relationship.

- **Rebuilt the app** to protect patient privacy, in a domain where handling that data correctly is a requirement rather than a preference.
- **Reworked the user experience** so the product was navigable by patients under real conditions, not only by clinicians who use it daily.
- **Maintained reliability** for clinician–patient communication, the part of the product that has to work every time.

## The outcome

A telehealth app that met a materially higher privacy bar and was easier for patients to actually use, maintained by one person across several years of platform changes.

Regulated products suit how I work. They reward people who have shipped through App Store review repeatedly and who treat privacy and data handling as design constraints from the beginning rather than as a compliance pass at the end.
